## 1. Elementary Transformations of Matrices

Elementary transformations are basic operations performed on rows or columns of a matrix. They do not change the **rank** of the matrix.

There are **three types** of elementary transformations.

### Row Transformations

| Type | Operation | Notation |
|------|-----------|----------|
| 1. Row interchange | Swap two rows | $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$ |
| 2. Row scaling | Multiply a row by a non-zero scalar $k$ | $R_i \rightarrow kR_i$, where $k \neq 0$ |
| 3. Row addition | Add a multiple of one row to another row | $R_i \rightarrow R_i + kR_j$, where $i \neq j$ |

### Column Transformations

| Type | Operation | Notation |
|------|-----------|----------|
| 1. Column interchange | Swap two columns | $C_i \leftrightarrow C_j$ |
| 2. Column scaling | Multiply a column by a non-zero scalar $k$ | $C_i \rightarrow kC_i$, where $k \neq 0$ |
| 3. Column addition | Add a multiple of one column to another column | $C_i \rightarrow C_i + kC_j$, where $i \neq j$ |

**Key point:**  
Every elementary transformation is reversible.  
If $B$ is obtained from $A$ by one elementary transformation, then $A$ can be obtained from $B$ by another elementary transformation.

## 2. Equivalent Matrices

Two matrices $A$ and $B$ of the same size are called **equivalent** if one can be obtained from the other by a finite sequence of elementary row and/or column transformations.

**Notation:**

$$A \sim B$$

This relation is an **equivalence relation** because it is:

- **Reflexive:** $A \sim A$
- **Symmetric:** If $A \sim B$, then $B \sim A$
- **Transitive:** If $A \sim B$ and $B \sim C$, then $A \sim C$

If only **row transformations** are used, the matrices are **row equivalent**, written:

$$A \sim_{\text{row}} B$$

If only **column transformations** are used, the matrices are **column equivalent**, written:

$$A \sim_{\text{col}} B$$

**Important result:**  
Equivalent matrices have the same rank.

## 3. Row Echelon Form (REF) and Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF)

### Row Echelon Form (REF)

A matrix is said to be in **row echelon form** if:

1. All zero rows (if any) are at the bottom of the matrix.
2. In each non-zero row, the first non-zero entry (called the **pivot** or **leading entry**) is to the right of the pivot of the row above it.
3. All entries below a pivot are zero.

**Example of REF:**

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Here the pivots are 1, 3, and 4.

### Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF)

A matrix is in **reduced row echelon form** if:

1. It is in row echelon form.
2. Every pivot is 1.
3. Each pivot 1 is the only non-zero entry in its column.

**Example of RREF:**

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Here the pivot columns are columns 1, 2, and 3, and each pivot 1 is the only non-zero entry in its column.

**Note:**  
Any matrix can be transformed into REF or RREF using a finite sequence of elementary row operations. RREF is unique for a given matrix, while REF is not unique.

## 4. Rank of a Matrix

The **rank** of a matrix $A$ is the number of non-zero rows in its row echelon form (or reduced row echelon form). It is also equal to:

- The maximum number of linearly independent rows of $A$.
- The maximum number of linearly independent columns of $A$.

**Notation:**

$$\operatorname{rank}(A)$$

or sometimes:

$$\operatorname{r}(A)$$

**Key facts:**

- $\operatorname{rank}(A)$ is always a non-negative integer.
- If $A$ is an $m \times n$ matrix, then $0 \leq \operatorname{rank}(A) \leq \min(m, n)$.
- Elementary transformations do **not** change the rank of a matrix. So, equivalent matrices have the same rank.

## 5. Finding the Rank Using REF

The rank of a matrix can be found by converting the matrix to **row echelon form (REF)** using elementary row operations, and then counting the number of non-zero rows.

### Steps

1. Write down the matrix $A$.
2. Use elementary row operations to transform $A$ into REF.
   - Swap rows if needed.
   - Scale rows to make pivots non-zero.
   - Add multiples of one row to another to create zeros below pivots.
3. Count the number of non-zero rows in the REF.
4. That number is $\operatorname{rank}(A)$.

### Worked Example

Find the rank of:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Step 1:** Start with $A$.

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Step 2:** Use $R_2 \rightarrow R_2 - 2R_1$ to eliminate the first entry of row 2.

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Step 3:** Use $R_3 \rightarrow R_3 - R_1$ to eliminate the first entry of row 3.

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Step 4:** Interchange row 2 and row 3 to bring the non-zero row upward.

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Now the matrix is in row echelon form.  
The non-zero rows are:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

There are **2 non-zero rows**.

Therefore:

$$\operatorname{rank}(A) = 2$$

### Another Example

Find the rank of:

$$B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

This matrix is already in REF.  
It has **2 non-zero rows**, so:

$$\operatorname{rank}(B) = 2$$
